# Moneyness and value

Terms describing where an option's strike sits relative to the underlying price, and how its premium splits into two parts.

**Moneyness** A description of the relationship between the strike price and the current price of the underlying.

**In the money** A call whose strike is below the underlying price, or a put whose strike is above it. Such an option has intrinsic value.

**At the money** An option whose strike is at or very near the underlying price.

**Out of the money** A call whose strike is above the underlying price, or a put whose strike is below it. Such an option has no intrinsic value.

**Intrinsic value** The amount by which an option is in the money. It cannot be negative; an out-of-the-money option has zero intrinsic value.

**Extrinsic value** The part of the premium beyond intrinsic value, sometimes called time value. It reflects the time remaining and the expected movement of the underlying, and it decays toward zero as expiration approaches.

**Premium decomposition** Premium equals intrinsic value plus extrinsic value. At expiration, extrinsic value is gone and only intrinsic value, if any, remains.
