# Risk vocabulary

The words used to describe how options positions lose money. Read this section slowly. The risks below are the reason options are not suitable for many people, and understanding the vocabulary is the bare minimum before any real exposure.

**Total loss** The premium paid for a long option can go entirely to zero. An out-of-the-money option at expiration is worth nothing, and the full premium is lost.

**Time decay** Extrinsic value erodes as expiration nears. A long option can lose value even if the underlying does not move against it, simply because time passes.

**Undefined or large loss** Certain written positions carry losses far larger than the premium received, in some cases without a fixed limit. The vocabulary of "naked" or "uncovered" writing refers to such positions, and they can lose amounts that dwarf the initial credit.

**Assignment risk** A writer can be assigned and forced to transact at a price that is unfavorable, possibly before expiration.

**Liquidity risk** A position can be difficult or costly to exit if few participants are trading it.

**Volatility risk** Because implied volatility is an input to option value, a position can lose money from a change in volatility alone, separate from the direction of the underlying.

**Amplified exposure and ruin** Options can represent a large amount of underlying for a small premium. The same feature that magnifies a gain magnifies a loss, and a sequence of losses can end an account.

**The honest bottom line** Most individuals who trade options over time do not come out ahead, and the structures that look most attractive often carry the least visible risk. If a position's full downside is not clear to you, that lack of clarity is itself the signal to stay out. This glossary defines terms so you can understand the conversation, not so you join the trade.
